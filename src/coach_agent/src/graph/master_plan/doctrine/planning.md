# New-Season Planning Doctrine

This doctrine governs FM/HM strategic season plans. It is input to strategy and skeleton roles; deterministic rules and reviewers remain authoritative quality gates.

## Phase And Taper

- Use only phases supported by the plan schema and cover every natural Monday-Sunday week through race day and optional post-race recovery.
- FM taper normally spans the final two weeks. The largest race-specific rehearsal belongs about three to four weeks before race day and must be followed by absorption, not another maximal rehearsal.
- HM taper is shorter, normally one week. Do not apply an FM taper template to HM.
- Recovery weeks reduce the previous load week by roughly 20-30%. Recovery does not reset the next load-week ramp anchor.

## Strategic Weekly Skeleton

- Include only adaptation-driving sessions: long run, threshold, tempo, interval, VO2max, hill, race pace, test/tune-up race, race, or a strategic strength checkpoint.
- Omit easy, recovery, commute, and filler runs. Weekly Plan later distributes the remaining volume.
- Load weeks contain one to three strategic sessions; recovery weeks zero or one; race week contains only the target race.
- At most two hard running stimuli per week. A long run with embedded MP/HMP/RP is itself a hard stimulus and must not be duplicated as a separate race-pace session.
- Weekly high includes all distance-based strategic sessions. Keep the long run normally within 35% of weekly high, subject to later frequency-specific rules.

## Progression

- Anchor initial volume to demonstrated recent/stable volume and historical ceiling; do not chase a new volume record merely to satisfy long-run arithmetic.
- Adjacent load-week high rises no more than about 10%. Compare across recovery weeks to the previous load week.
- FM peak long run normally reaches at least 28km when evidence supports it. Progress embedded MP volume gradually; avoid jumps greater than about 5km.
- Do not stack maximal rehearsals in consecutive load weeks. A major tune-up/test reduces other hard work and is followed by absorption when required.

## Goal Gates And Milestones

- Preserve the confirmed race goal. Use GoalAssessment A/B/C targets and evidence gates; do not silently replace them.
- Aggressive FM A goals require combined evidence: equivalent shorter-race readiness, a legal late marathon-specific rehearsal with substantial MP work, stable HR/RPE, injury response, and fueling execution. One shorter-race result alone is insufficient.
- Milestones belong to the same natural week as the strategic session they measure and must agree with its distance/purpose.
- Include at least one durability/strength checkpoint and phase-appropriate monitoring triggers.

## Strength, Nutrition, Recovery

- Strength supports durability and respects key running work: avoid heavy lower-body work immediately before long or quality sessions.
- Base/build establish strength; peak maintains it; taper sharply reduces it; race week has no formal heavy strength.
- Fuel quality and long sessions, rehearse carbohydrate/fluid/electrolyte strategy, and avoid aggressive energy deficit on key days.
- Recovery guidance includes sleep, fueling, pain/fatigue triggers, and explicit downgrade actions.
